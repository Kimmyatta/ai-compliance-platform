Human-centered design means taking into account a human-centered perspective that is iterative, addresses the whole user experience and involves relevant parties throughout design and development. It involves responsive and iterative design, validation, monitoring and communications. For example, information is more accessible and usable if the following are considered: • providing the appropriate level of detail for the intended audience. • arranging the content in order of importance or to best support the ability of users to process the information and make informed decisions. Plain language may be appropriate in some cases for understanding and usability. In other cases, technical language may be relevant for specialized clinical users.
